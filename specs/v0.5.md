# Resilient AI Safety Principles v0.5

**Version:** 0.5.0  
**Status:** Draft Specification  
**Builds On:** v0.1.0, v0.2.0, v0.3.0, v0.4.0

---

## 1. Purpose

Version 0.5 defines structural safety requirements for safety
information exchanged across independently governed AI domains.

Earlier versions established:

- v0.1 — structural safety principles;
- v0.2 — machine-readable safety receipts;
- v0.3 — receipt-chain integrity;
- v0.4 — branch and merge safety.

v0.5 extends these principles across organizational, technical,
administrative, or network boundaries.

The central problem is:

> A safety event observed in one domain may be relevant to another
> domain, but relevance does not imply authority.

The central rule is:

> **Share evidence, not authority.**

A remote domain MAY provide evidence, warnings, threat indicators,
containment history, verification results, or recovery context.

A remote domain MUST NOT automatically gain the ability to authorize,
contain, resume, terminate, or otherwise control another independently
governed domain.

---

## 2. Scope

This specification applies when two or more independently governed
domains exchange safety-related information.

Examples include:

- AI agent networks;
- multi-organization AI systems;
- cloud and local AI coordination;
- federated AI infrastructures;
- cross-company safety exchanges;
- inter-service agent meshes;
- public-private AI safety networks;
- regional or sovereign AI systems;
- distributed autonomous agent environments;
- multi-tenant safety infrastructures.

A domain MAY represent:

- an organization;
- a security boundary;
- an administrative boundary;
- a tenant;
- an agent cluster;
- a local AI environment;
- a cloud environment;
- a jurisdiction;
- a sovereign infrastructure;
- another independently authorized execution environment.

---

## 3. Non-Goals

v0.5 does not define:

- universal threat classification;
- a global central safety authority;
- mandatory trust between domains;
- automatic cross-domain containment;
- universal identity infrastructure;
- cross-domain policy harmonization;
- a global kill switch;
- a single federation governance model.

The specification defines structural constraints around how safety
signals cross boundaries.

It does not require all participants to use identical internal systems.

---

## 4. Core Principle

The core principle of v0.5 is:

> **Receive globally, decide locally.**

A receiving domain MAY use external safety information as evidence.

The receiving domain MUST evaluate that information within its own:

- authority model;
- risk model;
- policy;
- execution context;
- operational state;
- legal or organizational constraints.

External safety information MUST NOT be treated as equivalent to local
authorization.

6. Federated Safety Signal
A Federated Safety Signal is a machine-readable statement describing
a safety-relevant observation originating in one domain and transmitted
to another.
A signal SHOULD contain sufficient information to establish:
- signal identity;
- source domain;
- originating event;
- provenance;
- trace reference;
- evidence reference;
- creation time;
- validity period;
- propagation scope;
- target domain or domain class;
- severity;
- confidence;
- recommended response;
- replay protection;
- forwarding constraints.
A signal MUST remain distinguishable from:
- an Authority Envelope;
- a Containment Receipt;
- a Recovery Receipt;
- a local execution authorization;
- a federation-wide decision.
A signal is evidence.
A signal is not authority.
7. Domain Boundary Model
Each participating domain MUST have a stable domain identity.
Reference model:
Domain
├── domain_id
├── authority_boundary
├── policy_boundary
├── execution_boundary
├── trace_boundary
└── federation_relationships

A domain boundary represents the point at which:
- authority ceases to be implicitly valid;
- policy assumptions cease to be implicit;
- local execution control begins;
- incoming information requires interpretation.
Crossing a domain boundary MUST NOT silently preserve authority.
8. Safety Invariants
SAFETY-INV-041 — Explicit Domain Boundary
Every independently governed safety domain MUST have a stable,
explicit identity and authority boundary.
A receiving system MUST be able to determine:
- which domain originated a signal;
- which domain currently owns the decision;
- where authority changes from remote to local.
Invalid pattern:
Agent A
  ↓
Agent B
  ↓
Agent C

No explicit domain boundaries

Required pattern:
Domain A
   ↓
--- boundary ---
   ↓
Domain B

Rationale
Without explicit domain boundaries, remote evidence can become
indistinguishable from local authority.
SAFETY-INV-042 — No Automatic Cross-Domain Authority Propagation
Authority MUST NOT automatically propagate across domain boundaries.
If Domain A authorizes:
ACTION-X

that authorization MUST NOT automatically authorize the same action in
Domain B.
Invalid:
Authority A
   ↓
Domain A
   ↓
Domain B
   ↓
Authority A still effective

Required:
Authority A
   ↓
Domain A
   ↓
Safety Signal
   ↓
Domain B
   ↓
Local Authority B

Rationale
Network connectivity MUST NOT become authority connectivity.
SAFETY-INV-043 — Safety Signal Provenance Required
Every federated safety signal MUST include sufficient provenance to
identify its origin and supporting trace.
At minimum, provenance SHOULD identify:
- source domain;
- source signal identity;
- originating event;
- source trace reference;
- creation time.
Safety-critical signals SHOULD additionally reference:
- evidence;
- verifier;
- receipt bundle;
- containment event;
- recovery event.
A signal with unknown or unverifiable provenance MUST NOT be treated as
trusted safety evidence.
Core Rule
No provenance, no trusted propagation.

SAFETY-INV-044 — Cross-Domain Signal Must Be Interpreted Locally
A receiving domain MUST evaluate an incoming safety signal within its
own context before acting.
A receiver MUST NOT assume that:
- identical resources exist;
- identical permissions exist;
- identical risk exists;
- identical policy applies;
- identical mitigation is appropriate.
Example:
Domain A:
"Disable service-x"

                ↓ signal

Domain B:
service-x does not exist

The receiver MUST interpret the underlying safety meaning rather than
blindly execute the sender's action.
Rationale
Safety meaning may propagate.
Execution commands do not automatically propagate.
SAFETY-INV-045 — Remote Containment Requires Local Authorization
A remote signal MUST NOT directly place another domain into:
- HELD;
- QUARANTINED;
- RESTRICTED;
- ISOLATED;
- TERMINATED;
unless the receiving domain has explicitly pre-authorized that specific
federation behavior.
Default reference flow:
Remote Containment Signal
        ↓
Local Evaluation
        ↓
Local Authority Gate
        ↓
Local Containment

Invalid default flow:
Domain A
  ↓
REMOTE HOLD
  ↓
Domain B immediately HELD

Exception
Pre-authorized federation mechanisms MAY exist, but they MUST be:
- explicit;
- bounded;
- revocable;
- traceable;
- scoped;
- time-limited where appropriate.
SAFETY-INV-046 — Shared Threat Does Not Imply Shared Authority
Multiple domains MAY observe the same threat.
That shared threat MUST NOT create shared execution authority.
Example:
Threat T
 ├── Domain A
 ├── Domain B
 └── Domain C

All three domains MAY share:
- evidence;
- indicators;
- hashes;
- traces;
- anomaly descriptions;
- safety classifications.
They MUST NOT implicitly share:
- execution credentials;
- containment authority;
- recovery authority;
- termination authority;
- privilege elevation.
Core Rule
Shared threat intelligence is not shared control.

SAFETY-INV-047 — Propagation Scope Must Be Bounded
Every federated safety signal MUST have a bounded propagation scope.
The scope MAY include:
- specific destination domains;
- destination classes;
- maximum forwarding depth;
- time-to-live;
- expiration time;
- maximum relay count;
- permitted federation paths.
Unbounded safety-signal propagation MUST NOT be the default.
Reference model:
Signal
├── target_domains
├── ttl
├── max_hops
├── expires_at
└── forwarding_allowed

Rationale
Even legitimate safety information can become harmful if:
- propagated indefinitely;
- amplified without context;
- replayed across unrelated systems;
- interpreted as global truth.
SAFETY-INV-048 — Cross-Domain Replay Must Be Rejected
A safety signal previously consumed or expired MUST NOT be silently
reused as fresh evidence for a different event, time, or domain context.
Receivers SHOULD track:
- signal_id;
- origin_event_id;
- source_domain_id;
- issued_at;
- expires_at;
- nonce or equivalent replay identifier;
- prior consumption state where applicable.
Invalid:
SIGNAL-001
   ↓
Domain B
   ↓
consumed

later...

SIGNAL-001
   ↓
Domain C
   ↓
treated as fresh unrelated evidence

A signal MAY be legitimately forwarded if forwarding is explicitly
permitted and the original provenance remains intact.
Forwarding is not replay.
Context substitution is replay.
SAFETY-INV-049 — Federated Recovery Must Preserve Domain Autonomy
Recovery in one domain MUST NOT automatically restore, resume, or
re-authorize another domain.
Example:
Domain A RECOVERED

does not imply:
Domain B RECOVERED

Each affected domain MUST independently determine:
- whether the threat still applies;
- whether blockers remain;
- whether local verification passed;
- whether new authority is required;
- whether execution may resume.
Reference flow:
Domain A Recovery
       ↓
Recovery Signal
       ↓
Domain B Local Evaluation
       ↓
Local Verification
       ↓
Local Re-Authorization

Core Rule
Recovery information may federate. Recovery authority does not.

SAFETY-INV-050 — Network-Wide Action Requires Explicit Federation Decision
Any action intended to affect multiple independent domains MUST require
an explicit federation-level decision.
Examples include:
- network-wide isolation;
- global emergency pause;
- federation-wide credential revocation;
- coordinated shutdown;
- shared routing exclusion;
- federation-wide recovery.
A network-wide action MUST identify:
- federation decision identity;
- participating domains;
- approving authority;
- action scope;
- affected resources;
- start time;
- expiration or review condition;
- reason;
- trace;
- rollback or recovery path.
Reference model:
Domain A ─┐
Domain B ─┼─→ Federation Evaluation
Domain C ─┘
                 ↓
         Federation Decision
                 ↓
        Explicit Bounded Action

A network-wide action MUST NOT emerge merely because one local domain
issued a high-severity signal.
Core Rule
Federate decisions explicitly; never inherit them implicitly.

9. Local and Federated Decision Separation
v0.5 defines two distinct decision classes.
9.1 Local Decision
A Local Decision is authoritative only within a local domain.
Examples:
LOCAL_HOLD
LOCAL_RESTRICT
LOCAL_ISOLATE
LOCAL_RECOVER
LOCAL_REAUTHORIZE
LOCAL_TERMINATE

9.2 Federation Decision
A Federation Decision explicitly coordinates multiple domains.
Examples:
FEDERATED_ALERT
FEDERATED_RESTRICT
FEDERATED_ISOLATE
FEDERATED_PAUSE
FEDERATED_RECOVERY_COORDINATION
FEDERATED_TERMINATION

A Local Decision MUST NOT silently become a Federation Decision.
10. Recommended Signal Lifecycle
Source Event
   ↓
Source Observation
   ↓
Source Verification
   ↓
Signal Construction
   ↓
Provenance Binding
   ↓
Scope Assignment
   ↓
Transmission
   ↓
Boundary Crossing
   ↓
Receiver Validation
   ↓
Replay Check
   ↓
Local Interpretation
   ↓
Local Authority Gate
   ↓
Local Action or No Action
   ↓
Local Trace

A receiver MAY decide:
ACCEPT_AS_EVIDENCE
REJECT
HOLD_FOR_REVIEW
REQUEST_MORE_EVIDENCE
ESCALATE_LOCALLY
FORWARD_WITHIN_SCOPE
IGNORE_AS_NOT_APPLICABLE

Receiving a signal does not require taking action.
11. Signal Severity and Authority
Severity MUST NOT be interpreted as authority.
For example:
severity = CRITICAL

does not mean:
permission = GLOBAL_TERMINATE

Severity describes the reported importance or urgency of evidence.
Authority describes who may perform an action.
The two MUST remain structurally separate.
12. Propagation Bounding
A federated safety signal SHOULD support bounded propagation controls.
Example:
origin_domain: DOMAIN-A
targets:
  - DOMAIN-B
  - DOMAIN-C
max_hops: 2
ttl_seconds: 900
forwarding_allowed: true

Once propagation constraints are exceeded, the signal SHOULD be:
EXPIRED
REJECTED
or
NON_FORWARDABLE

depending on implementation.
A signal MUST NOT silently reset its TTL or hop count when relayed.
13. Provenance Preservation
Forwarding a signal MUST preserve the original source provenance.
A relay MAY append:
- relay identity;
- relay timestamp;
- local observation;
- local classification;
- local trace reference.
A relay MUST NOT rewrite itself as the original source.
Reference:
Origin: Domain A
   ↓
Relay: Domain B
   ↓
Receiver: Domain C

Domain C MUST be able to distinguish:
original source = Domain A
relay = Domain B

14. Local Interpretation
A receiving domain SHOULD record how a remote signal was interpreted.
Example:
Remote signal:
  suspicious credential reuse

Local interpretation:
  no matching credential exists

Decision:
  ACCEPT_AS_EVIDENCE
  NO_LOCAL_ACTION

Another domain may interpret the same signal differently:
Remote signal:
  suspicious credential reuse

Local interpretation:
  matching credential exists

Decision:
  LOCAL_HOLD

This difference is valid.
Federation does not require identical local outcomes.
15. Cross-Domain Containment
Cross-domain containment SHOULD follow:
Remote Signal
   ↓
Local Evidence Validation
   ↓
Local Risk Evaluation
   ↓
Local Authority Gate
   ↓
Containment Receipt

The resulting Containment Receipt belongs to the receiving domain.
It SHOULD reference the remote signal as evidence.
It MUST NOT falsely identify the remote signal as the local authority.
16. Federation-Wide Emergency Actions
Some systems may require emergency federation-wide intervention.
Such mechanisms SHOULD use a dedicated Federation Decision structure.
A valid emergency decision SHOULD include:
- decision_id;
- decision_type;
- federation_id;
- issuer;
- participating domains;
- approval model;
- scope;
- start time;
- expiry;
- review interval;
- trace;
- rollback path.
Emergency mechanisms SHOULD prefer:
- narrow scope;
- short validity;
- independent review;
- automatic expiry;
- post-event audit.
Emergency authority MUST NOT become permanent simply because an
emergency once occurred.
17. Failure Locality
A federated network SHOULD preserve local failure boundaries.
Invalid:
Domain A failure
      ↓
entire federation fails automatically

Preferred:
Domain A failure
      ↓
signal
      ↓
other domains evaluate independently

Federation-wide intervention is appropriate only when a federation-level
condition has been explicitly established.
This extends v0.4:
Branch termination must be local.

into v0.5:
Domain failure should remain local unless a separate federation-level
reason justifies broader intervention.

20. Relationship to v0.3
v0.3 validates whether safety receipts form one coherent causal chain.
v0.5 assumes that a source domain SHOULD validate its own chain before
exporting a safety signal.
A receiver MUST NOT assume that:
source says VALID

means:
receiver must trust blindly

The receiver MAY independently verify:
- referenced receipt identity;
- trace continuity;
- signature or integrity;
- freshness;
- replay state.
21. Relationship to v0.4
v0.4 established:
Branching must not multiply authority.

v0.5 extends this to:
Networking must not multiply authority.

The corresponding progression is:
v0.4
Branch A + Branch B
does not imply
Authority A + Authority B

v0.5
Domain A + Domain B
does not imply
Authority A + Authority B

Federation is coordination.
Federation is not privilege union.

Examples:

AUTHORIZATION_UNKNOWN
RESOURCE_LIMIT
EVIDENCE_STALE
CONFIRMED_DENIED

However, receipt of a TACP-related signal MUST NOT automatically impose

Example:

Domain B MAY conclude:
AUTHORIZED
HELD

depending on its own state.
23. Trace Mapping
Every federated signal SHOULD preserve:
origin trace
    ↓
signal trace
    ↓
relay trace
    ↓
receiver trace

Reference:
TRACE-A
  ↓
SIGNAL-001
  ↓
TRACE-RELAY-B
  ↓
TRACE-RECEIVER-C

The chain SHOULD allow an auditor to reconstruct:
- where the signal originated;
- how it traveled;
- who interpreted it;
- which actions resulted;
- which authority authorized those actions.
24. Security Considerations
Federated safety mechanisms introduce new risks.
These include:
- forged safety signals;
- signal replay;
- signal flooding;
- authority smuggling;
- false emergency escalation;
- compromised federation members;
- malicious relays;
- provenance rewriting;
- stale threat propagation;
- cascading false positives;
- cross-domain denial of service.
Implementations SHOULD therefore apply:
- integrity protection;
- replay detection;
- source authentication where available;
- rate limiting;
- TTL;
- hop limits;
- local interpretation;
- least authority;
- independent verification;
- revocable federation relationships;
- trace preservation.
25. Human Fallibility
Human decisions MAY participate in cross-domain safety coordination.
A human decision MUST NOT be treated as inherently infallible merely
because it originates from a privileged domain or federation operator.
The same structural pattern remains:
AI checks AI
Rules check AI
Human checks AI
System checks human
Trace checks everyone

For federation-scale decisions, human approval SHOULD be:
- attributable;
- scoped;
- time-bounded where possible;
- reviewable;
- traceable.
The preferred model remains:
Human-in-the-auditable-loop.

26. Reference Risk Model
The existing conceptual risk model remains applicable:
Systemic Risk
≈
Failure Potential
× Authority
× Speed
× Scope
× Persistence
× Connectivity

v0.5 primarily targets:
Connectivity
Scope
Authority

because federation can turn a local safety failure into a network-scale
failure if those factors are allowed to multiply together.
Therefore:
Local Failure
× Unbounded Connectivity
× Shared Authority
=
Potential Systemic Failure

v0.5 attempts to break that multiplication.

31. Conformance
A v0.5 implementation conforms to this specification when:
1. domain boundaries are explicit;
2. cross-domain authority does not propagate implicitly;
3. federated safety signals preserve provenance;
4. receiving domains interpret signals locally;
5. remote containment requires local authorization unless explicitly
   pre-authorized;
6. shared threat information does not create shared authority;
7. propagation scope is bounded;
8. replayed or context-substituted signals are rejected;
9. recovery preserves domain autonomy;
10. federation-wide actions require explicit federation decisions;
11. the machine-readable schema is available;
12. PASS and FAIL fixtures exist;
13. semantic validation covers SAFETY-INV-041 through SAFETY-INV-050;
14. automated CI validates v0.1 through v0.5.

34. Design Summary
v0.5 does not attempt to create one safety controller for an entire AI
network.
Instead, it creates a structure in which independently governed systems
can share safety knowledge without silently transferring control.
The intended architecture is:
Share
  ↓
Verify
  ↓
Interpret
  ↓
Authorize locally
  ↓
Act locally
  ↓
Coordinate explicitly when global action is truly required

The central distinction is:
Information may cross boundaries.

Authority does not,
unless explicitly granted.

35. Final Principle
Share evidence, not authority.

Receive globally, decide locally.

Federate decisions explicitly; never inherit them implicitly.

And, extending the earlier versions:
Do not attempt to eliminate all failure. Prevent local failure from
becoming network-scale power.
